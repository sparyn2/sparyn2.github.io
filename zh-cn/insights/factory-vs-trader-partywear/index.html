Factory vs Trader for Partywear

How to tell whether your sequin eveningwear supplier owns production control—or only brokers POs.

Mar 15, 2026 · Sourcing

The real risk in partywear sourcing

Sequin eveningwear has more process steps than basic jersey dresses: pattern, sequin application, lining, stretch recovery, and delicate packing. When a trader sits between you and the line, feedback loops lengthen and bulk drift becomes common—especially on Fashion Nova or SHEIN-speed calendars.

Signals of a production-capable partner

  • Can share in-line photos of cutting, sequin work, and sewing for your style.
  • Owns or tightly controls sample room turnaround (days, not weeks).
  • Speaks clearly about AQL, needle detection, and carton standards.
  • Can explain why a sequin map was changed between SMS and bulk.
  • Offers one project contact accountable for cost, quality, and ship date.

Signals you are talking to a pure trader

  • Vague answers on which workshop does sequin application.
  • Samples arrive with different fabric handfeel than the quoted bulk mill.
  • Cannot provide packing photos until goods are already sealed.
  • Price jumps after “factory confirmation” without a clear BOM change.

What platform vendors should require

  1. Written SMS approval with measurement sheet.
  2. Locked BOM including sequin lot and lining.
  3. Mid-production photo or video update.
  4. Pre-shipment AQL report and carton count.
  5. Reorder archive so season two matches season one.
